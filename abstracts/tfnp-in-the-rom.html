We determine which major \(\mathrm{TFNP}\) classes are hard in the random oracle model (ROM). Prior work establishes average-case hardness of highly structured subclasses of \(\mathrm{TFNP}\) from strong cryptographic assumptions such as LWE and indistinguishability obfuscation. It is not known whether weaker assumptions such as one-way functions imply worst-case hardness of any \(\mathrm{TFNP}\) problem. The ROM, where all parties have query access to a random function, lies, morally, between these two regimes: a random function is one-way, but public-key cryptography cannot be built from it in a black-box way. We refer to this setting as Romantica.

Our first result settles a long-standing open problem: \(\mathrm{PPAD}\), the class of total search problems reducible to finding a Nash equilibrium, is hard on average in Romantica. Combined with the known hardness of \(\mathrm{PLS}\) (local search), this gives average-case hardness of \(\mathrm{EoPL}=\mathrm{PPAD}\cap\mathrm{PLS}\). In contrast, we show that \(\mathrm{UEoPL}\) can be solved in Romantica with polynomially many oracle queries by a computationally unbounded solver, a sharp distinction between \(\mathrm{EoPL}\) and \(\mathrm{UEoPL}\). This resolves the black-box hardness landscape of all major \(\mathrm{TFNP}\) classes in Romantica.

Finally, we initiate the study of separations between \(\mathrm{TFNP}\) subclasses in Romantica: \(\mathrm{PWPP}\) and \(\mathrm{PPA}\) do not reduce to \(\mathrm{PLS}\) under public-coin reductions.
