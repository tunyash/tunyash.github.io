We make a number of contributions to the complexity of sampling distributions: new general tools, lower bounds and upper bounds.

A main contribution is a separation between DNFs of size \(n^c\) and decision trees of depth \(n^c\). Previous separations were either for logarithmic-depth trees, or for distributions not samplable in \(\mathrm{AC}^0\). As an application, we obtain new data-structure lower bounds for \(\mathrm{AC}^0\) problems. Along the way we prove a direct product theorem for sampling by decision trees: if a distribution is hard to sample, then many independent copies are exponentially harder to sample.

We also show how to reduce the input length of a sampler to nearly minimum with a small increase in complexity. For example, surprisingly we show that any distribution sampled in \(\mathrm{AC}^0\) can be sampled in \(\mathrm{AC}^0\) using only \(n+c\) input bits, up to error \(0.01\). Previous results gave \(n\log^c n\) input bits. For \(\mathrm{NC}^0\) we can reduce to \((1+\epsilon)n\).

We also show that for every symmetric function \(f\) on \(n-1\) bits, the distribution \((x,f(x))\) for uniform \(x\) can be sampled in \(\mathrm{AC}^0\) exactly, whereas previous samplers had errors. Finally, we give a new lower bound for sampling a uniform string of weight \(k\), simplifying previous work and extending it to the cell-probe model.
